#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
This preprint tackles a persistent bottleneck in acoustic-emission (AE) monitoring: conventional piezoelectric transducers are hard to multiplex over large structures, while fiber-optic AE schemes have been held back by interrogation complexity and limited wavelength resolution. The authors interrogate a fiber Bragg grating with a hybrid III-V/silicon-nitride (SiN) integrated laser whose linewidth is below 10 kHz and whose emission wavelength is tunable at sub-picometer precision. Crucially, a machine-learning model calibrates and stabilises this tuning, converting the laser into a high-resolution, self-correcting interrogator. The combination lets a compact, chip-scale source track the minute Bragg-wavelength shifts that acoustic waves impress on the grating, delivering the sensitivity usually reserved for bulky benchtop interrogators. The significance is twofold. First, moving the narrow-linewidth source onto a photonic-integration platform is a concrete step toward scalable, low-cost, autonomous sensing nodes rather than laboratory prototypes. Second, embedding ML directly in the calibration loop addresses the drift and nonlinearity that normally erode tunable-laser interrogation, hinting at sensor networks that self-maintain in the field. For readers tracking the convergence of silicon photonics, fiber sensing and edge AI, this is an unusually complete demonstration spanning device physics to system autonomy, and it maps a credible route toward distributed photonic SHM networks that operate continuously with minimal human oversight.

#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
This preprint reframes city-scale DAS as a network-coverage problem and asks a deceptively simple question: how much of a city's fiber must be instrumented before different monitoring tasks become feasible? By treating buried telecom fiber as a spatial sensing graph, the authors identify task-dependent coverage thresholds. Strikingly, they show that even sparse coverage below 10% — if the instrumented routes are chosen well — is sufficient for high-value applications such as earthquake early warning, groundwater monitoring, geological mapping and coarse urban-activity tracking. They then borrow the language of percolation theory to identify a sharp transition at 51.6% coverage, above which the sensing graph becomes effectively continuous across the city and fine-grained statistical traffic monitoring becomes possible. The contribution is conceptual rather than instrumental: instead of another hardware or algorithm improvement, it offers a planning framework that tells municipalities and network operators where the diminishing-returns and phase-transition points lie when rolling out DAS on dark fiber. That matters because DAS deployments are ultimately constrained by which fibers are available, not by interrogator performance. The percolation framing gives a principled target for infrastructure investment and a way to reason about redundancy and gaps. Readers in urban geophysics, smart-city sensing and telecom-asset reuse will find the thresholds directly actionable for deployment strategy.

#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
DAS turns a single seafloor cable into thousands of strain channels, but the resulting data deluge — continuous strain over tens of kilometres — has outpaced manual interpretation. This preprint introduces DASNet, an end-to-end deep-learning framework that automates the three tasks that gate any monitoring pipeline: detecting events, classifying their type, and picking arrival times. The system is validated at scale on three years of continuous recordings from SeaFOAM, a seafloor fiber-optic array in Monterey Bay, where it catalogued more than 500,000 events across several signal categories (spanning marine biological, oceanographic and seismic sources). The headline is not a single new network architecture but the demonstration that a learned pipeline can convert raw, noisy, multi-year DAS into a structured, searchable event catalogue with minimal human labelling — a prerequisite for treating dark submarine fiber as a permanent geophysical and ecological observatory. Methodologically the work is important because marine DAS mixes very different signal classes with strongly varying signal-to-noise, so a model that generalises across them is non-trivial. The scale of the catalogue also enables downstream statistical study (event rates, spatial distribution) that would be impossible by hand. Limitations typical of learned detectors apply: label quality, class imbalance and transfer to other cables. Still, for ocean-bottom seismology, marine mammal monitoring and offshore infrastructure surveillance, DASNet is a concrete template for operational, automated DAS analytics.

#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
Spin-based quantum sensors — most prominently nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centres in diamond — deliver exquisite magnetic, electric and thermal sensitivity, but scaling and integrating them is hampered by how their spins are controlled: conventional setups deliver microwaves over bulky coax and antennas that are hard to route, isolate and multiply. This preprint proposes carrying that control signal optically instead, presenting a unified framework for RF-over-fiber (RFoF) control of optically accessible spins. The idea is to modulate the microwave drive onto an optical carrier, distribute it over fiber, and recover it at the sensor — decoupling the control electronics from the sensing head and exploiting fiber's low loss, electromagnetic immunity and routing flexibility. The authors demonstrate the concept with RFoF optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) of NV centres, the canonical readout that maps spin resonances to fluorescence. For the fiber-sensing community this sits at an interesting frontier: it brings quantum sensors closer to the same fiber-networked, remoted architecture that classical distributed sensors already enjoy, enabling EMI-immune probes at the end of long fiber links and, potentially, multiplexed quantum sensor arrays. It is an early, enabling demonstration rather than a fielded sensor, and questions of RFoF link noise, phase stability, power budget and how far the fiber can run before control fidelity degrades remain to be quantified. Still, as a bridge between quantum sensing and fiber photonics it is a forward-looking, cross-disciplinary contribution worth watching.

#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
Interferometric fiber sensors ultimately convert a measurand (temperature, strain, refractive index) into a wavelength shift that an interrogator must resolve, so any scheme that magnifies that shift for the same physical perturbation is valuable. This preprint cascades two classic elements in series — a multimode interference (MMI) sensor and a Fabry-Perot interferometer (FPI) — so that their different spectral periodicities interact. When two periodic spectral responses of slightly different free spectral range overlap, the combined envelope moves much faster than either component (a Vernier-like amplification), which sharpens the effective wavelength-shift resolution beyond what either MMI or FPI achieves alone. The appeal is practical: both MMI and FPI structures are simple, robust, all-fiber and cheap to fabricate, so improving resolution by cascading rather than by adding expensive high-resolution interrogation hardware is attractive for cost-sensitive deployments. The work is an incremental but sound contribution in the well-trodden interferometric-sensor line; its value is in the specific in-series MMI+FPI configuration and the demonstrated resolution gain. Trade-offs typical of Vernier schemes apply and should be checked in any adoption: amplified sensitivity usually shrinks the unambiguous measurement range, envelope demodulation can be more complex, and cross-sensitivity (e.g. temperature vs strain) is not automatically resolved. For engineers building compact, low-cost interferometric probes who need finer resolution without a spectrometer upgrade, it is a useful design pattern.
