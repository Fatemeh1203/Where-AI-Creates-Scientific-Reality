### 1. Computation in flight through precision-processed optical fiber
**عنوان (فارسی):** Computation in flight through precision-processed optical fiber

- **Authors / نویسندگان:** Kailai Liu, Zhongyao Luo, Xiangpeng Xiao, Zhen Wang, Weiliang Zhao, Mingming Zhang, Hao Wu, Zhijun Yan, Ming Tang

- **Open Access:** Yes (preprint) · **Citations:** — · **IF:** — · **Quartile:** —
- **Research Area / حوزه:** Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG)
- **Keywords / کلیدواژه‌ها:** physics.optics
- **Rating / امتیاز:** ★★★☆☆ (3/5)

#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
In distributed sensing and computing systems, optoelectronic conversion, buffering, and scheduling can make data movement a bottleneck. We propose FBG-CA, a passive computing module fabricated by automated through-coating inscription of weak fiber Bragg gratings (FBGs). The spatially distributed FBGs reflect, delay, and accumulate temporal samples to produce a convolution output in the backward direction, while the forward signal carries the original data stream to a remote receiver. This enables transmission and convolution within the same optical propagation event. To mitigate finite reflectivity resolution and fabrication deviations, we combine quantization-aware training with sharpness-aware minimization. Over 1440 m of computing fiber, the single-wavelength implementation achieves a convolution correlation coefficient of 0.987, classification accuracies of 87.90% on MNIST and 79.07% on MIT-BIH Arrhythmia, and an estimated throughput of 288 GOPS. With approximately 20% forward-power reduction (0.97 dB) across the FBG sections, the recovered MNIST images achieve a mean structural similarity index of 0.9930, and the recovered MIT-BIH heartbeat waveforms have a mean squared error of 0.005826. Using four wavelength-division-multiplexed channels over 144 m of computing fiber, FBG-CA achieves 92.25% accuracy on four-class Fashion-MNIST and an estimated throughput of 115.2 GOPS. An architecture-level projection indicates an intrinsic throughput density of 1.6 POPS/m for eight wavelengths at 100 GBaud per wavelength. These results demonstrate passive, wavelength-scalable computation-in-flight in optical fiber.

### 2. A feasible point method for derivative-free linearly constrained optimization

- **Keywords / کلیدواژه‌ها:** math.OC
- **Rating / امتیاز:** ★★★★☆ (4/5)

#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
This paper presents a framework for linearly constrained derivative-free optimization (DFO) in which the original objective function is evaluated only at feasible points. The method first analyzes the linear constraint system using certificates of infeasibility and implied activity to detect infeasibility or a singleton feasible set, or, when the feasible set has positive dimension, to identify the affine subspace spanned by the feasible set and obtain an equivalent reduced representation with a strictly feasible base point. It then transforms the resulting reduced problem into an unconstrained optimization problem by means of a pointed feasible retraction and an exact penalty function. The retraction maps arbitrary trial points to feasible points by an explicit scaling in the reduced coordinates, so that no constrained optimization or projection subproblem is required during objective evaluations. This construction permits a broad range of unconstrained DFO methods to be used as inner solvers while preserving feasibility of the original objective evaluations. Numerical experiments compare several such variants on linearly constrained test problems of different dimensions. The results show that the choice of inner solver affects robustness, the number of problems solved, function-evaluation efficiency, and computational time, and that relative performance changes with problem dimension.

### 3. Monogenic signal analysis for wavefield characterisation in distributed acoustic sensing
**عنوان (فارسی):** Monogenic signal analysis for wavefield characterisation in distributed acoustic sensing

- **Authors / نویسندگان:** Aleix Segui, Arantza Ugalde, Andreas Fichtner, Huynh Van Luong, Wes Armour
- **Affiliation / وابستگی:** — · **Country / کشور:** —
- **Venue / منبع:** arXiv (preprint) — arXiv · **Preprint**
- **Published / انتشار:** 2026-11-22 · **DOI:** `10.48550/arXiv.2611.29757`
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29757v1 · **Scholar:** [search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Monogenic+signal+analysis+for+wavefield+characterisation+in+distributed+acoustic+sensing)
- **Open Access:** Yes (preprint) · **Citations:** — · **IF:** — · **Quartile:** —
- **Research Area / حوزه:** Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS)
- **Keywords / کلیدواژه‌ها:** physics.geo-ph
- **Rating / امتیاز:** ★★★★☆ (4/5)

#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
The spatio-temporal wavefield sampled by distributed acoustic sensing (DAS) contains intertwined information about signal strength, oscillatory structure and propagation. We introduce a methodology to disentangle these properties, with applications to the analysis of weak arrivals obscured by noise or stronger wave trains. The method is based on the monogenic signal, a two-dimensional extension of the analytic signal defined via the Riesz transform. In DAS, the monogenic signal decomposes locally dominant arrivals into their amplitude, phase and apparent slowness along the fibre. Moreover, the consistency of the wavefield orientation, quantified using the notion of directional coherency, allows coherent arrivals to be identified and enhanced. In this work, we apply the monogenic signal to analyse submarine T-wave recordings following the magnitude 3.5 earthquake of 5 October 2023 in the western Alboran Sea, acquired along approximately 60 km of the Estepona-Tetouan fibre-optic cable. Directional coherency allows us to enhance coherent wave trains, attenuating the incoherent background while preserving their oscillatory structure. Combining the monogenic attributes, we track individual oscillations across the fibre, obtaining spatially consistent apparent-slowness estimates. Together with varying cable geometry, these estimates allow us to infer the incoming directions of two wave trains, revealing distinct backazimuths. We extend the methodology to direction-selective enhancement, revealing a weaker third wave train with opposite moveout that is obscured by stronger arrivals. Beyond these examples, we show that monogenic analysis offers new possibilites for wavefield comparison, with promising applications to lossy data compression and inversion. Our results highlight the versatility of monogenic analysis and its potential to become a central tool for interpreting DAS recordings.
