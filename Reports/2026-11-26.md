#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
Distributed acoustic sensing (DAS) transforms optical fibres into dense arrays of vibration sensors for continuous, long-range monitoring with high spatial resolution. Yet converting these large-scale measurements into actionable information with compact models that retain task performance remains challenging. Existing artificial-intelligence approaches are typically optimized for specific sensing environments and tasks, but their computational requirements can hinder deployment in compact, power-constrained field systems. Here, we introduce DAS-NanoNet, a framework that automatically constructs lightweight neural networks for different DAS scenarios and tasks, together with OmniDAS, an open benchmark spanning six representative environments. Using lightweight temporal operators tailored to DAS signals, an adaptive neural architecture search strategy derives a compact network for each scenario-task pairing. Across classification, localization, compression, and denoising tasks in diverse DAS applications, DAS-NanoNet consistently yields highly compact architectures. On a six-class dataset, it achieves 99.68% accuracy with only 1,391 trainable parameters, while delivering comparable or better performance with up to 18,098-fold fewer parameters than previously reported high-accuracy networks. Its compact architecture further enables edge deployment, achieving sub 9 $μ$s inference latency on the deployed hardware platform across different DAS applications. DAS-NanoNet provides a route towards edge intelligence for compact, low-power and real-time DAS systems through ultracompact networks tailored to individual sensing scenarios and tasks.

#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
Waveguide-based Raman spectroscopy offers a route toward compact and integrated photonic sensors. While most waveguide Raman platforms operate in the near-infrared, extending excitation toward the visible and ultraviolet is attractive because shorter excitation wavelengths can provide substantially stronger Raman scattering and access to resonance-enhanced molecular responses. This transition, however, places greater demands on the photonic material because of optical loss, absorption and waveguide-generated spectral background. Here, we investigate aluminum oxide ($Al_2O_3$) waveguides as a low-background platform for Raman spectroscopy at 532 nm and 325 nm using a back-collected excitation and detection geometry. At 532 nm, after normalization to the incident excitation power-time product, the measured $Al_2O_3$ background was more than two orders of magnitude lower than that of $Si_3N_4$. Under 325 nm excitation, the waveguides maintained a weak and reproducible spectral background across the investigated widths. Using immersion oil as a model analyte deposited over the waveguide surface, we demonstrate that analyte-specific Raman fingerprints can be recovered above the waveguide background at both wavelengths. Signal-to-background ratio and spectral contrast were used to quantify analyte visibility relative to the waveguide background, and both metrics consistently confirmed clear detection of the analyte bands. These results demonstrate that $Al_2O_3$ combines low measured spectral background with operation from the visible into the ultraviolet, providing a promising route toward integrated Raman spectroscopy at shorter excitation wavelengths.

### 3. Generalized Disjunctive Programming with Derivative-Free Configuration Search
**عنوان (فارسی):** Generalized Disjunctive Programming with Derivative-Free Configuration Search

- **Authors / نویسندگان:** Albert Joon Lee, Woosik Kim, David E. Bernal Neira
- **Affiliation / وابستگی:** — · **Country / کشور:** —
- **Venue / منبع:** arXiv (preprint) — arXiv · **Preprint**

- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05508v1 · **Scholar:** [search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Generalized+Disjunctive+Programming+with+Derivative-Free+Configuration+Search)
- **Open Access:** Yes (preprint) · **Citations:** — · **IF:** — · **Quartile:** —
- **Research Area / حوزه:** Distributed Fiber Optic Sensing (DFOS)
- **Keywords / کلیدواژه‌ها:** math.OC
- **Rating / امتیاز:** ★★★☆☆ (3/5)

#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
Generalized disjunctive programming (GDP) describes process design alternatives with Boolean variables, disjunctions, and logical propositions, and fixing one configuration leaves a subproblem in the continuous variables. Conventional GDP methods require explicit equations, which a simulator may not expose, and mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) reformulations can be intractable for nonconvex models. The external-variable reformulation turns configuration choice into an optimization over an integer lattice whose objective is the subproblem value, which derivative-free optimization (DFO) can search while the GDP model continues describing the alternatives. We pair GDP models with surrogate DFO methods and the direct-search method NOMAD and compare them with discrete steepest descent (D-SDA), logic-based and MINLP solvers, and enumeration on nine process models, 21 test functions, a pharmaceutical flowsheet simulated in PharmaPy, and a catalytic distillation column with 100,947 configurations. On the largest reactor network, the surrogate methods MISO and RBFOpt reach the reference in nine or ten of ten runs with about 30 subproblem solves, whereas logic-based methods solve nearly one subproblem for each admissible configuration and most D-SDA descents end at one local minimum. In the flowsheet example, where GDP solvers do not apply, RBFOpt and MISO find the best of 36 simulated configurations in every run with 20 evaluations. For the catalytic column, D-SDA and MISO reach the best known design with 100 evaluations, whereas MINLP solvers find no design within 15% of it and global solvers return contradictory certificates. This search pays off when the configuration space is too large to enumerate, has deceptive local minima, or has MINLP reformulations that fail, and enumeration or a direct reformulation is simpler for small algebraic models.

#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
This paper presents a framework for linearly constrained derivative-free optimization (DFO) in which the original objective function is evaluated only at feasible points. The method first analyzes the linear constraint system using certificates of infeasibility and implied activity to detect infeasibility or a singleton feasible set, or, when the feasible set has positive dimension, to identify the affine subspace spanned by the feasible set and obtain an equivalent reduced representation with a strictly feasible base point. It then transforms the resulting reduced problem into an unconstrained optimization problem by means of a pointed feasible retraction and an exact penalty function. The retraction maps arbitrary trial points to feasible points by an explicit scaling in the reduced coordinates, so that no constrained optimization or projection subproblem is required during objective evaluations. This construction permits a broad range of unconstrained DFO methods to be used as inner solvers while preserving feasibility of the original objective evaluations. Numerical experiments compare several such variants on linearly constrained test problems of different dimensions. The results show that the choice of inner solver affects robustness, the number of problems solved, function-evaluation efficiency, and computational time, and that relative performance changes with problem dimension.

#### 📝 Summary / خلاصه
The spatio-temporal wavefield sampled by distributed acoustic sensing (DAS) contains intertwined information about signal strength, oscillatory structure and propagation. We introduce a methodology to disentangle these properties, with applications to the analysis of weak arrivals obscured by noise or stronger wave trains. The method is based on the monogenic signal, a two-dimensional extension of the analytic signal defined via the Riesz transform. In DAS, the monogenic signal decomposes locally dominant arrivals into their amplitude, phase and apparent slowness along the fibre. Moreover, the consistency of the wavefield orientation, quantified using the notion of directional coherency, allows coherent arrivals to be identified and enhanced. In this work, we apply the monogenic signal to analyse submarine T-wave recordings following the magnitude 3.5 earthquake of 5 October 2023 in the western Alboran Sea, acquired along approximately 60 km of the Estepona-Tetouan fibre-optic cable. Directional coherency allows us to enhance coherent wave trains, attenuating the incoherent background while preserving their oscillatory structure. Combining the monogenic attributes, we track individual oscillations across the fibre, obtaining spatially consistent apparent-slowness estimates. Together with varying cable geometry, these estimates allow us to infer the incoming directions of two wave trains, revealing distinct backazimuths. We extend the methodology to direction-selective enhancement, revealing a weaker third wave train with opposite moveout that is obscured by stronger arrivals. Beyond these examples, we show that monogenic analysis offers new possibilites for wavefield comparison, with promising applications to lossy data compression and inversion. Our results highlight the versatility of monogenic analysis and its potential to become a central tool for interpreting DAS recordings.
